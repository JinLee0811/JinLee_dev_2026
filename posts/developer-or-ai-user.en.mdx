When ChatGPT first came out, I caught on pretty quickly. 🤖

That was about three to five years ago.
Most people around me weren't talking about AI yet,
but I was already tinkering with it, sensing that something was about to fundamentally shift.

---

## 🫤 Using It Without Trusting It

That said, I was far from a true believer in the beginning.

Every time it generated code, I'd scrutinize each line — "Is this actually right?"
Every answer it gave, I'd approach with at least half a degree of suspicion.

I was in this strange middle ground: **recognizing AI as a useful tool, yet never fully trusting it.**

But my reliance grew, naturally, year by year.

---

## 🚫 The Phase Where I Tried to Step Away

At some point, I made a deliberate effort to pull back.

> "Code written with AI isn't really my skill."
> "Relying on AI for development won't help me in job interviews."

These ideas crept in — and honestly, they got to me.
I started feeling a kind of pressure to type everything by hand, debug on my own,
prove I could do it without assistance.

---

## 💡 One YouTube Video Changed My Mind

Then I watched a few videos from Silicon Valley engineers.

They had already fully integrated AI into their workflow.
Claude Code, Cursor, multi-agent setups, token optimization — all of it was just part of how they built.

That's when it hit me.
**The idea that "real developers don't use AI" wasn't a principled stance — it was a dated one.**

The game had already changed. I was the one holding onto the old rulebook.

---

## 📉 The Job Market in 2026

I graduated with my Master's this year and jumped into the job search.

The feeling is different from what I expected.
**There are fewer roles.** Fewer companies to apply to.

You can sense the cost-cutting happening everywhere.
AI adoption is reducing the headcount that companies once needed.

The old formula — "graduate, and the opportunities will come" — doesn't hold the same way anymore.
Increasingly, how well you can work alongside AI has become one of the most relevant things you can demonstrate.

---

## ⚙️ What Happened After I Set Up Claude Code

These days, instead of watching videos on development techniques,
I'm mostly watching content about what new AI tools have dropped,
what features got added, and especially — how to get the most out of Claude Code.

Efficient token usage. Better prompting for better outputs.

But as always, **ten videos are no substitute for actually doing it.**

Over the last two or three days, I installed Claude Code on my laptop and got my setup dialed in. 🛠️
I'm currently running five agents in parallel,
and after trying out the remote feature, I realized I don't really need to install Open Claw separately anymore.

---

## 🌀 More Work, Not Less

I assumed AI would make things easier. Make things lighter.

The opposite happened.

**I fell into a kind of productivity spiral.** 😅
Build one thing, immediately see the next thing to build.
Solve one problem, immediately spot the next one.

The urge to keep creating just compounds.
And somewhere in the middle of it all, I started thinking seriously about launching something of my own.

---

## 🧠 The Most Important Thing I've Learned

Using Claude Code made one thing very clear to me:

> **AI-assisted work is only as effective as the knowledge you bring to it.**

When I'm working within a domain I understand well, I can lead the AI completely —
set the direction, evaluate the output, and drive toward the next step.

But the moment I push past the edge of what I know,
**I'm no longer leading the AI. The AI is leading me.**

This is exactly why domain knowledge and fundamentals still matter,
even — maybe especially — in an era dominated by AI tools.

There's too much to learn and too much to know.
But honestly? That's what makes it interesting.

not as a casual observer, but as someone actively using and experimenting with it throughout.

I don't just use the tools. I think about how to use them well.

**I'm ready to bring that into a real team, working on a real product.**
